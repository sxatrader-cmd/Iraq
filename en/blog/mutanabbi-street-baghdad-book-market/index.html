Al-Mutanabbi Street in Baghdad: the book street, Friday market and Shabandar Café

Book stalls, the Friday market, tea at Shabandar Café and the poet's statue by the Tigris: how to enjoy Baghdad's famous book street.

If you love bookshops, Al-Mutanabbi Street in Baghdad will feel like coming home. This narrow, car-free lane in the old heart of the city has been the place where Iraqis buy, sell, swap and argue about books for generations. On Friday mornings it turns into a great open-air book market, and you suddenly understand why Baghdad is known as a city that reads.

In this guide you will find where Mutanabbi Street is, the poet behind its name, what to see on the way down to the Tigris and how to get the most out of your visit, with tips from Saif, your local guide in Baghdad.

Where is Al-Mutanabbi Street in Baghdad?

Al-Mutanabbi Street lies on the Rusafa side of Baghdad, the east bank of the Tigris, right in the historic centre. It is a narrow pedestrian street, just under a kilometre long, that branches off al-Rasheed Street, the old main avenue of Baghdad, and runs down towards the river, ending in a small square with a view over the water.

That location makes it the perfect starting point for exploring old Baghdad on foot. Several of the city's most interesting historic places are within a few minutes' walk, so you can easily spend a whole morning here without getting into a car.

The history of Baghdad's book street

An Abbasid inheritance

According to tradition, the book trade in this quarter goes back to the Abbasid era, when Baghdad was one of the world's great centres of learning. It was the age of the House of Wisdom, of translators and copyists, and of the warraqin, the paper-makers and book-sellers who produced and sold handwritten volumes. Walking between the stalls today, it is easy to imagine that long line running from then to now.

The poet behind the name

The street received its present name in 1932, under King Faisal I, in honour of the poet Abu al-Tayyib al-Mutanabbi. Born around 915 in Kufa and died in 965, he is considered one of the greatest poets in the history of the Arabic language. His verses are famous for their pride and sharp wit, and many of his lines have become sayings that Iraqis still quote every day. It says a lot about Baghdad that its book street carries his name.

"Cairo writes, Beirut prints, Baghdad reads"

A well-known saying in the Arab world divides the roles this way. It is meant with a smile, but on Mutanabbi Street it rings true. Here reading is not a solitary hobby; it is something people share, discuss and celebrate together.

What to see on Mutanabbi Street

The entrance arch and the bookshops

You enter through an arch decorated with lines from the poet's work. Beyond it, bookshops, stationers, printers and second-hand stalls line both sides of the street. You will find school books and dictionaries, poetry, history, philosophy and old magazines. The street has been renovated in recent years, with new stone paving, lighting and restored façades, and it is now a real pleasure to stroll.

The Friday book market

The highlight is Friday morning. Second-hand books are spread out on the pavements and the street fills with families, students, poets, artists and musicians. People read aloud, debate and strike up conversations over piles of books. The atmosphere feels more like a cultural festival than a market.

The statue of al-Mutanabbi

At the end of the street stands a bronze statue of the poet, made in 1977 by the Iraqi sculptor Mohammed Ghani Hikmat. It overlooks the Tigris from a small square and is one of the most photographed spots in old Baghdad, especially under a deep blue sky with the clock tower of the Qishla behind it.

Shabandar Café and the Qishla

A tea house for writers

Near the bottom of the street, close to the Qishla, is Shabandar Café, founded in 1917. It is a legendary meeting place for writers and intellectuals, its walls covered with old black-and-white photographs of Baghdad. Order strong tea in a small istikan glass and sit among the regulars: there are few better ways to feel the city's cultural life.

The Qishla by the river

The Qishla is an Ottoman-era government building from the second half of the 19th century, with a clock tower about 22 metres high. Today it is a cultural space with gardens that reach down to the Tigris, a lovely place to rest in the shade after browsing.

Nearby sights in old Baghdad

Mutanabbi Street combines easily with other places in the old city, all within walking distance:

Souq al-Saray

Right next to the street is Souq al-Saray, an old market of around 300 metres selling stationery and books, entered near the al-Saray Mosque, built in 1660.

Al-Mustansiriya Madrasa

Founded by the caliph al-Mustansir in the 1220s–1230s, this Abbasid school is considered one of the oldest universities in the world and is the natural next stop for anyone fascinated by the history of learning.

The Baghdadi Museum and Souq al-Safafeer

The Baghdadi Museum recreates traditional city life, while Souq al-Safafeer, the coppersmiths' market, is known for the sound of hammers on metal.

Best time to visit the Baghdad book market

Friday morning is without doubt the best time, when the street is at its liveliest and the famous book market is in full swing. On other days it is quieter, which can be nice if you want to browse at leisure. For the season, the cooler months from October to April are the most pleasant for exploring the city on foot.

Practical tips for your visit

  • Arrive early on Friday to see the market before it gets really busy.
  • Bring cash in small notes; it makes buying from the stalls much easier.
  • Wear comfortable shoes: the street is pedestrian and you will walk a lot in the old city.
  • Ask before photographing people; most are friendly, but it is polite.
  • Dress neatly and modestly, with shoulders and knees covered, as elsewhere in Baghdad.
  • Leave time for a glass of tea at Shabandar Café; it is part of the experience.
  • Entry rules for Iraq change from time to time, so check the latest official visa information before booking.
